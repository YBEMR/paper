1.   Introduction and Scope
     The Hailo-8™ AI Acceleration Modules are a comprehensive family of PCI
     Express (PCIe) based acceleration modules that meet industry standards for a
     range of form factors and performance objectives, targeted at artificial
     intelligence (AI) applications.
     This application note applies to the following products:
     •   Hailo-8™ M.2 Key M AI Acceleration Module
     •   Hailo-8™ M.2 Key A+E AI Acceleration Module
     •   Hailo-8™ M.2 Key B+M AI Acceleration Module
     •   Hailo-8™ M.2 Key M ET AI Acceleration Module
     •   Hailo-8™ M.2 Key A+E ET AI Acceleration Module
     •   Hailo-8™ M.2 Key B+M ET AI Acceleration Module
     Due to its high performance and small form factor, most practical applications
     require any of the Hailo-8™ modules to have a heat dissipation solution – to
     avoid an overheat condition and a thermal shutdown. It is critical that the
     hosting enclosure/chassis vendor provide a proper thermal solution.
     This application note details the design considerations of the required thermal
     solution and provides some tested “recipes” for typical system configurations
     to reduce thermal design overhead.

3.     Thermal Requirements and
       Specifications
3.1.   Heat Dissipation
       The heat created in the Hailo-8™ AI Acceleration Module is mostly dissipated
       to the top surface of the Hailo-8™ device on the top side of the board, where
       it can be further conducted to a heat sink or other heat transfer solution.
       The Hailo AI Acceleration Module will also dissipate some heat via the module
       PCB, edge connector and socket, mounting screw and stand-off. However, this
       dissipation is small compared with the heat dissipation from the chip’s top
       surface. From here on, we will neglect this heat dissipation path, which will
       result in a small positive margin for the thermal design. The smaller the
       module’s form factor, the smaller this margin.
       The module’s power consumption is highly dependent on the resources
       utilized for inference, and the operating frame rate or input rate used.
       The junction temperature relates to the ambient temperature according to the
       equation 𝑇𝐽 ≈ 𝑇𝐴 + 𝑃 ∙ (𝜃𝑗𝑐 + 𝜃𝑐𝑎 ), where heat dissipation via the PCB is
       neglected and:
       •   𝑇𝐽 is the Hailo-8™ silicon junction temperature
       •   𝑇𝐴 is the ambient air temperature
       •   P is the power dissipated by the module
       •   𝜃𝑗𝑐 is the thermal resistance from junction to device case (package top
           surface) and is an inherent property of the device
       •   𝜃𝑐𝑎 is the thermal resistance from the Hailo-8™ device case to ambient air.

3.3.   Thermal PCIe Shutdown
       The Hailo-8™ firmware automatically shuts down the PCIe data lanes when a
       predefined temperature is crossed to prevent the junction temperature from
       rising above the absolute maximum rated (AMR).


3.4.   Thermal Throttling
       The thermal throttling mechanism main purpose is to keep the Hailo-8™
       device from overheating during operation and exceeding absolute maximum
       ratings. In addition, the user API allows it to customize some of the parameters
       of solution in order to comply to specific usage model.
       Throttling mechanism is enabled by default on Hailo modules but can be
       deactivated by the system integrator. While it operates, the temperature
       sensors embedded in the chip silicon are configured to interrupt the ARM
       Cortex M4 executing Hailo FW when a temperature threshold (dubbed Orange
       threshold temperature) is crossed.
       When the temperature threshold cross is detected, Hailo FW starts reducing
       the clock frequency of the chip, which should reduce dynamic power of the
       chip and hopefully its junction temperature.
       In addition, when the temperature sensor exceeds a defined Red Threshold
       Temperature the ARM Cortex M4 is interrupted again, and in this situation,
       Hailo FW is closing the data interface to the host to drastically reduce the
       power consumption and the junction temperature.
       Between Orange and Red thresholds, the chip’s junction temperature is
       constantly monitored by periodically reading the measured temperature.
       In this domain there are up-to 7 additional calculated threshold – dubbed

TH[i]. Please note that TH[0] = Orange Threshold.

4.     Typical System Configurations
       This section presents 3 different heat dissipation solutions.


4.1.   Natural Convection Solution
       In this configuration, the AI Acceleration Module is
       mounted in a chassis that is allows ample ventilation
       and free air flow (an open frame computer, for
       example).
       There should be little or no obstruction between the
       module and ambient air. The ambient environment
       should be such that ambient temperature is                                     Figure 3 - Natural convection conditions
       unaffected by the system’s heat dissipation.


       Under these conditions, the air flow around the module is small (typically less
       than 0.5 meters per second) and is mostly due to thermal convection.
       The thermal solution required for this configuration is the attachment of a
       heat sink with a large surface area, on top of the Hailo-8™ device, using a
       thermally conductive pad and mounting screws on opposite sides of the
       device.
       In this configuration, 𝜃𝑐𝑎 is the sum of:
       1. The thermal resistance of the thermal pad
       2. The thermal resistance between the heat sink bulk surface and ambient
          air.

4.3.   Fanless Enclosure (Direct Conductance)
       In this configuration, the AI Acceleration Module is
       enclosed in a closed-box chassis that allows little or no
       air movement. A fanless mini-computer and some
       industrial/ruggedized computer models fit into this use
       case category.
       In this configuration, a thermal conductor (typically a

       from the Hailo-8™ device to the enclosure surface,

       1. The thermal resistance of a thermal pad 1mm thick (1.7 ℃⁄𝑊 ), used to
          interface the Hailo-8™ top lid to the aluminum bar
