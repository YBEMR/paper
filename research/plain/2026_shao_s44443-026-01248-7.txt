Multi‑objective reinforcement learning scheduling framework
for heterogeneous AI chips: Energy‑efficient and latency‑aware
edge‑cloud collaboration
Shijia Shao1 · Peiqing Ye2 · Biao Zhao3 · Hao Lu4
Received: 9 May 2026 / Accepted: 12 August 2026
© The Author(s) 2026

Abstract
Scheduling AI inference across heterogeneous edge-cloud chips requires balancing energy, latency, cost, and thermal
feasibility. This study presents a chip-aware scheduling framework, rather than a new multi-objective reinforcement
learning theory. A 53-dimensional state describes directed-acyclic-graph tasks, CPU/GPU/NPU/FPGA status, network
conditions, and queue slack. A fuzzy controller adjusts energy, latency, and cost priorities, while a hybrid-action Proximal
Policy Optimization policy jointly selects the offloading target, physical chip, and dynamic-voltage-and-frequency-scaling
coefficient. Dependency, deadline, thermal, and bandwidth constraints are enforced through action masks and residual
penalties. In traffic-video and industrial-inspection simulations, the framework achieved 5.8 TOPS/W, 132 ms average latency,
a normalized cost coefficient of 0.17 per task, and 85.2% Pareto coverage. Under an equal 1000-episode and 30-seed budget,
PPO reached 95% of its asymptotic improvement in 580 ± 45 episodes, obtained a final normalized return of -0.15 ± 0.03
and an HV of 0.87 ± 0.02, and produced no divergent seed. EdgeCloudSim/iFogSim2 replication and hardware-in-the-loop
testing preserved the advantage over DTRL; in hardware-in-the-loop tests, the framework required 31.5 ± 3.5 J per task and
142 ± 8.0 ms, compared with 35.8 ± 4.0 J and 155 ± 9.5 ms for DTRL. The results indicate that task-aware objective adaptation
and chip-level control can reduce wasteful offloading and thermal stress while preserving service timeliness.
Keywords Heterogeneous AI chips · Edge-cloud collaboration · Multi-objective reinforcement learning · Dynamic voltage
and frequency scaling · Thermal-aware scheduling · Sustainable computing

Artificial intelligence (AI) services are increasingly deployed
across edge devices and cloud data centers. This edge-cloud
structure can reduce response time by processing urgent tasks
near the data source while using cloud accelerators for compute-intensive subtasks (Luo and Dai 2024; Li and Wu 2023).
However, heterogeneous AI chips such as CPUs, GPUs, NPUs
and FPGAs differ substantially in computing capability, power
consumption, thermal threshold, programming overhead and
network access cost. A scheduler that only minimizes latency
may overuse high-power cloud GPUs, whereas a scheduler that
only minimizes energy may delay safety–critical tasks (Bai
et al. 2024; Li et al. 2025). Therefore, the central problem of
this manuscript is how to make real-time scheduling decisions
that jointly reduce energy consumption, maintain deadline
satisfaction, control chip temperature and avoid unnecessary resource cost in heterogeneous edge-cloud collaboration
(Abbache, 2025; Rajesh and Pyne 2022).

Existing scheduling methods do not fully solve this
problem. Static hardware-performance rules cannot adapt
to sudden workload changes or bandwidth fluctuation.
Single-objective deep reinforcement learning (DRL) can
generate online decisions, but it usually optimizes one target and therefore hides the conflict among energy, latency
and cost (Gu et al. 2025; Naik and Alam, 2025; Ma and
Hou 2025; Zhang, 2025). Adaptive MORL and preferenceconditioned policies improve objective weighting, but many
of them focus on preference exploration or computation
offloading and do not explicitly combine task type, chiplevel thermal-frequency dynamics, and link bandwidth in
one action space (Han et al. 2025; Pournazar et al., 2025;
Basaklar et al. 2023; Miao et al. 2025). This gap is important
for sustainable computing because unnecessary offloading,
overheating and repeated migration increase energy use and
may shorten device life.
To address the above gap, the proposed framework
answers three research questions. First, how can task
descriptors, heterogeneous chip states and network
states be represented as a compact online scheduling
state? Second, how can the scheduler adapt the relative
priority of energy, latency and cost according to task
urgency and hardware conditions without relying on fixed
manual weights? Third, how can a reinforcement learning
policy jointly decide offloading location, chip selection
and DVFS-level frequency adjustment while respecting

deadline, thermal and bandwidth constraints? The answers
to these questions define the technical contribution of the
manuscript and avoid presenting the work as a general

The contribution is an engineered scheduling framework with four jointly implemented components, not a
new MORL theory. (1) A chip-aware 53-dimensional
state model integrates workload size, DAG dependency,
utilization, temperature, normalized resource price, bandwidth, and queue slack. (2) A fuzzy controller converts
task urgency, energy sensitivity, and cost sensitivity into

while dependency, deadline, thermal, and bandwidth
masks remove infeasible choices. (4) A completed verification protocol combines matched-budget actor-critic

reference-set construction, network/hardware robustness
tests, EdgeCloudSim/iFogSim2 replication, hardware-inthe-loop measurements, and resource-pricing sensitivity.
The technical novelty therefore lies in the coordinated
chip-aware scheduling design and its constraint-handling
integration, rather than in claiming a new general-purpose
scalarization or reinforcement-learning principle. Figure 1

2 Research progress on heterogeneous
computing scheduling
and multi‑objective optimization
2.1 Heterogeneous computing scheduling
Heterogeneous computing scheduling aims to coordinate
the efficient collaboration of processors with different
architectures (such as CPU, GPU, NPU) to meet the
demands of diverse computing tasks (Kazemi et al.,
2025). Early research mainly relied on static strategies,
such as pre-defined rules based on hardware performance
databases to allocate computationally intensive tasks
to GPUs and low-power tasks to CPUs. Although this
type of method can simplify the scheduling process, it is
difficult to adapt to dynamic load changes and hardware
state fluctuations (Nagabushnam and Kim 2025; Zheng
et al. 2025). In recent years, dynamic deep reinforcement
learning (DRL) has gradually become a research hotspot,
which dynamically adjusts task allocation strategies by
sensing real-time chip computing power, temperature,
and energy consumption data. For example, some
scholars have proposed scheduling algorithms based on
Q-learning, which generate real-time decisions based on
task computation and current chip load, achieving a 20%
reduction in latency in image processing scenarios (Jiang
et al., 2025). However, existing DRL methods often focus
on a single optimization objective, such as minimizing
latency or energy consumption, and lack the global
balancing ability for multi-objective conflicts, leading to
resource imbalance in edge-cloud collaboration scenarios
(Karishma and Kumar 2025; Saad et al., 2025).

2.2 Edge‑cloud collaborative optimization
The core of edge-cloud collaborative optimization lies
in balancing the benefits of local computing and remote
offloading, and its research path can be divided into two
categories: energy efficiency priority and latency priority
(Peng et al. 2024). Energy efficiency priority methods
typically reduce system energy consumption through
task compression, computation offloading, and hardware
sleep mechanisms, such as migrating high-energy model
training tasks to the cloud and performing only lightweight
inference at the edge (Cheng et al. 2025; Dui et al. 2024;
Yang et al., 2024). This type of method shows significant
performance in improving the battery life of IoT devices,
but may lead to a surge in communication latency due to
excessive reliance on the cloud (Zeng et al. 2024). The
delay priority strategy emphasizes real-time performance,
such as prioritizing the allocation of critical tasks to edge

Page 3 of 21

825

nodes in autonomous driving scenarios, but its high
computing power requirements may lead to overheating
or excessive energy consumption of edge devices (Du
et al. 2023). Recent studies have attempted to integrate
two types of strategies, such as threshold based dynamic
switching mechanisms, but these methods rely on manual
parameter tuning and are difficult to adapt to complex
and changing network environments and task loads, and
have not fully considered the collaborative potential of
heterogeneous chips (Yang et al. 2023).

2.3 Multi‑objective reinforcement learning
Multi-objective reinforcement learning provides a new
approach for complex decision-making problems by
simultaneously optimizing multiple competitive objectives
(Nguyen et al. 2025). Traditional methods often use
linear scalarization, such as weighting delay, energy
consumption and cost into a single reward value, but their
weight settings rely on prior knowledge and are difficult
to adapt to dynamic scenes (Jang et al. 2025; Parhizi
et al. 2025). In contrast, Pareto-frontier-based distributed
reinforcement learning can generate a set of non-dominated
solutions for decision makers to choose according to their
preferences. For example, some studies use evolutionary
algorithms to generate Pareto-optimal solution sets
and then use reinforcement learning to select dynamic
strategies (Wang et al. 2025). However, such methods have
high computational complexity and have not fully solved
the nonlinear coupling among objectives. In edge-cloud
collaborative scenarios, network bandwidth fluctuations
and hardware heterogeneity further increase the difficulty
of multi-objective optimization, and existing methods still
have a significant gap between real-time responsiveness and
solution-set diversity (Zheng et al. 2025; Han et al. 2025).

2.4 Positioning relative to adaptive MORL
and heterogeneous scheduling
Recent adaptive MORL studies have introduced preference
vectors, decision-tree policies, adaptive scalarization and
transfer-based offloading. The proposed framework is
therefore positioned more narrowly as a heterogeneous
AI-chip scheduler for edge-cloud inference workloads, not
as a new general-purpose MORL theory. Its distinction lies
in the combination of four elements in one online loop:
task-type fuzzy inference, explicit NPU/GPU/FPGA/CPU
hardware profiles, thermal-frequency prediction under
DVFS, and bandwidth-aware offloading. Existing adaptive
MORL methods usually adapt preferences or offloading
decisions, whereas this framework also selects the physical
chip and the frequency level and then evaluates feasibility

Green cloud multimedia networking extends energy-aware
resource management from compute servers to network
functions and forwarding devices. NFV virtualizes functions
that would otherwise be tied to dedicated appliances, while
SDN exposes a programmable, logically centralized view of
traffic paths and link states. The GreenVoIP framework, for
example, coordinated virtualized service functions, servers,
and switches to reduce the number of active devices while
maintaining service quality (Montazerolghaem et al. 2020).
This line of work is directly relevant to the sustainability
motivation of the present study because communication
energy, path selection, and network-device activation
can offset the gains obtained from chip-level DVFS and
accelerator selection.
Research on task scheduling and load balancing in SDNbased cloud computing similarly uses controller visibility of
topology, link utilization, and server load to allocate tasks,
minimize response time, and improve resource utilization.
The recent review by Mahdizadeh et al. organizes rulebased, metaheuristic, and reinforcement-learning approaches
and emphasizes the interaction between task scheduling,
load balancing, and network-resource optimization
(Mahdizadeh et al. 2025). Compared with those studies, the
current environment does not implement an SDN controller
or virtual-network-function placement; it treats residual
bandwidth, round-trip time, and packet loss as observed
network states.
The two research directions are complementary rather
than competing. A cross-layer extension could expose SDN
path energy, congestion, and VNF-placement state to the
chip-aware scheduler and could add route or service-chain
selection to the action vector. Such an extension would
permit joint optimization of network paths, compute-node
selection, and chip frequency. The present manuscript limits
its claims to compute-side scheduling and identifies SDN/
NFV-aware orchestration as a future integration path.

by integrating the low-latency response capabilities of edge
devices with the high-performance computing power of the
cloud. However, the dynamic characteristics of heterogeneous hardware resources make it difficult for traditional static
modeling methods to accurately describe the system state,
and there is an urgent need for a dynamically aware architecture model (Constantin et al. 2023).

3.3 Design of Multi‑Objective Reinforcement
Learning Scheduling Framework
Multi-objective reinforcement learning provides a datadriven solution for complex scheduling problems by
autonomously exploring the mapping relationship between
environmental states and actions. However, traditional
deep reinforcement learning methods face challenges such
as high-dimensional state space, difficulty in discretizing
action space, and multi-objective conflicts in heterogeneous
scenarios.
3.3.1 Overall architecture
The state space integrates four categories: task descriptor,
node status, network descriptor and queue descriptor. The
task descriptor includes workload, input size, output size,
deadline, task type and DAG level; the node status includes
compute capability, utilization, power, temperature and
unit cost for each of the eight nodes; the network descriptor
includes bandwidth, round-trip time (RTT) and packet-loss
indicator; and the queue descriptor includes ready-queue
length, critical-path slack and remaining DAG ratio. The
action space is hybrid: one discrete offloading target, one
discrete chip index and one bounded continuous DVFS coefficient. This design is necessary because sustainable scheduling must decide not only where a task is executed but also
which chip executes it and at what frequency (Fig. 4).

3.3.3 Complexity, training overhead, and simulator
interface
With a shared 53–128-128 encoder, an eight-node categorical head, a four-chip categorical head, a two-parameter
Gaussian DVFS head, and one critic output, the reference
implementation contains 25,359 trainable parameters:
23,424 in the encoder and 1,935 in the output heads. One
forward decision has complexity O(dh + h^2 + hA), where
d = 53, h = 128, and A = 15 is the combined output size.
Feasibility masking adds O(|V| +|E_DAG|) work for node
checks and ready-task dependency checks. A PPO update
with K optimization epochs and minibatch size B has dominant complexity O(KBh^2). Supplementary Table S5 separates neural-kernel microbenchmarks from the 1.8 ± 0.2 ms
end-to-end decision latency, which additionally includes
state preprocessing, mask construction, action sampling,
and framework synchronization.

applies assignment and DVFS, advances the event queue,

4.1 Experimental setup
The evaluation uses two representative edge-cloud
inference workloads: intelligent transportation video
analysis and industrial defect detection. The goal is not to
claim universal coverage of all edge-cloud applications,
but to test the proposed scheduler under two contrasting
DAG patterns: a latency-sensitive video workflow and a
compute-dense industrial inspection workflow. To reduce
the risk created by a custom simulator, all baselines use
identical workload files, bandwidth traces, random seeds
and initial node states. Additional appendix tables are
prepared for broader traffic patterns, bandwidth volatility
and temperature-threshold sensitivity.
The current evaluation uses a self-developed Python/
PyTorch discrete-event environment. Its hardware profile
is derived from reported accelerator-level capability,
power ranges, and safe-temperature thresholds. Resource
price is represented by the normalized coefficient defined
in Sect. 3.2 and must not be interpreted as direct cloud
billing. The temperature update follows the DVFS-aware
one-step model, while edge-edge, edge-cloud, and cloudside links are sampled separately with round-trip-time and
packet-loss descriptors. Edge-cloud uplink and downlink
capacities are sampled independently, and communication
delay is calculated from residual rather than nominal
bandwidth after concurrent transfers are allocated. Each
episode contains 200 scheduling decisions per scenario,
learning-based methods are trained for 1000 episodes, and
statistical reporting uses 30 independent paired seeds.

4.3 Result analysis
4.3.1 Overall performance comparison
To evaluate the global optimization capability of the
framework, the comparative experiment evaluates
mainstream algorithms across three objective dimensions:
energy efficiency, latency and cost. The experimental
results show that the proposed method, through dynamic
weight allocation and cross-layer collaborative scheduling,
significantly outperforms traditional single-objective
reinforcement learning and static evolutionary algorithms
in terms of solution-set distribution breadth and objectivebalancing accuracy. The edge-cloud collaboration strategy
effectively suppresses resource fragmentation, while
the priority adjustment mechanism driven by the fuzzy
logic controller ensures dynamic adaptation between task
characteristics and hardware states.

The overall comparison shows that the proposed
framework balances the three objectives better than the
evaluated single-objective, static, and rule-based baselines.

adaptation, whereas NSGA-II has reasonable coverage
but slower online response. Edge-first scheduling overuses
local nodes, and cloud-first scheduling increases latency
and normalized cost. Under the protocol in Sect. 4.2, the
remaining 14.8% denotes feasible pooled reference points
not weakly dominated by the proposed policy; it does not
denote accepted infeasible schedules.

5 Discussion
The results suggest that sustainable edge-cloud scheduling
must be treated as a coupled decision problem rather than as
separate energy, latency or cost minimization. The proposed
framework improves the balance among objectives because
it senses task urgency, chip temperature, bandwidth and
resource price before selecting the offloading target, chip and
frequency (Mohanakumar et al. 2025). The matched actorcritic comparison, external simulator replication, hardwarein-the-loop measurements, and failure tests consistently preserve the advantage over the strongest reported baseline. The
evidence is nevertheless bounded by two workload families,
an eight-node default topology, and the specific hardware
and network traces used in the study.
Practical deployment remains challenging. First, hardware-in-the-loop testing captures real execution and replayed
network effects but not the full variability of production drivers, multi-tenant interference, thermal inertia, or providerside throttling. Second, communication overhead increases
with scale because state collection, action distribution, and

Journal of King Saud University Computer and Information Sciences

(2026) 38:825

bandwidth monitoring add signaling traffic; Appendix A6
reports the signaling-overhead results, and Appendix A10
shows end-to-end decision time rising to 6.5 ms at 64 nodes.
Third, the evaluated independent failures do not cover correlated rack, switch, power, or control-plane faults. The results
should therefore be interpreted as evidence of improved
resilience under defined disturbances, not as a production
reliability guarantee.
The SDN/NFV literature also clarifies the scope of the current findings. The present scheduler observes network conditions but does not control routes, virtual network functions,
or switch activation. Integrating its chip-level decisions with
an SDN controller could expose path congestion and network
energy to the state, while NFV orchestration could jointly
choose service-chain placement and accelerator assignment.
Such cross-layer control may improve end-to-end sustainability, but it would enlarge the action space and require new
stability, signaling-overhead, and failure-recovery analysis
(Montazerolghaem et al. 2020; Mahdizadeh et al. 2025).
The normalized price model permits relative resourcecost optimization while avoiding a universal cloud-billing
claim. Appendix Table A12 reruns the scheduler under five
price profiles. Relative to the current normalized profile, the
on-demand accelerator scenario increases the proposed cost
to 0.22 ± 0.03 and reduces cloud-GPU selection by 15%;
adding a 5% preemption-risk penalty shifts critical subtasks
away from spot GPUs; adding a USD 0.05/GB egress tariff increases data-local execution by 20%; and annualized
private-edge pricing raises local low-power resource utilization by 10%. Because provider rates depend on region,
SKU, contract, and date, these values are scenario-specific
sensitivity results rather than general price forecasts.
Two emerging directions deserve acknowledgement. Federated deep reinforcement learning can coordinate offloading
policies across distributed edge domains without centralizing
raw local data, although non-IID updates and communication
overhead introduce new constraints (Zhao et al. 2024). LLMbased schedulers can provide high-level reasoning and crossscenario generalization, but their output reliability, inference
cost, and enforceability of hard resource constraints remain
open concerns (Pei et al. 2025). These approaches complement
the current policy rather than invalidate it: federated learning
could distribute policy training, and an LLM could propose
high-level preferences, while the chip-aware constrained controller retains final executable control.
To improve reproducibility, the supplementary material
specifies the DAG workload generator, hardware profile,
bandwidth trace generator, PPO network configuration,
pseudo-random integer seeds, baseline adaptation rules,
statistical tests and result-file schema. The seed list should
use 30 non-date-based integer seeds generated from a fixed
pseudo-random generator so that reviewers can reproduce the pairing design without confusing seed identifiers

restricted by institutional policy, the manuscript does not
claim full open-source reproducibility. Instead, it provides
enough parameter and table information for an independent researcher to reconstruct the environment and verify the
reported protocol.

6 Conclusion
This study presents a thermal-aware, chip-aware scheduling
framework for heterogeneous AI accelerators in edge-cloud
collaboration. RQ1 is answered by the 53-dimensional state
that combines DAG-task descriptors, eight-node hardware
status, network conditions, and queue slack. RQ2 is answered
by the fuzzy controller, which converts task urgency, energy
sensitivity, and cost sensitivity into normalized dynamic
weights without claiming a new MORL scalarization theory.
RQ3 is answered by the hybrid-action PPO policy, which
jointly chooses offloading target, physical chip, and DVFS
coefficient while dependency, deadline, thermal, and bandwidth masks enforce feasibility. Under the default workloads, the framework achieves 5.8 TOPS/W, 132 ms average
latency, a normalized cost coefficient of 0.17 per task, and
85.2% Pareto coverage. The matched-budget comparison
further shows a final PPO return of −0.15 ± 0.03, HV of
0.87 ± 0.02, and zero divergent seeds, while external simulator and hardware-in-the-loop results preserve lower energy,
latency, and violation rates than DTRL.
The sustainability significance lies in reducing wasteful
remote offloading, avoiding overheated chips, improving use
of low-power edge accelerators, and balancing normalized
resource cost with deadline satisfaction. Under the tested disturbances, the framework degrades more slowly than DTRL,
including 210 versus 240 ms latency at 25% bandwidth and
65 versus 120 ms recovery latency when 25% of nodes are
unavailable. Pricing sensitivity also confirms that the policy
changes node selection when compute, interruption, egress,
or private-edge charges change. Future work should extend
the evidence to more workload families, larger and decentralized topologies, correlated hardware/network failures,
SDN/NFV-aware cross-layer orchestration, federated policy
learning, LLM-assisted high-level resource reasoning, privacy, security, and reliability as explicit objectives.
