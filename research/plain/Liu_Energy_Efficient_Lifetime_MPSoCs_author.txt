Energy-Efficient Application Mapping and
Scheduling for Lifetime Guaranteed MPSoCs
Weichen Liu, Member, IEEE, Juan Yi, Mengquan Li, Peng Chen, Lei Yang

Abstract—Energy optimization is one of the most critical objectives for the synthesis of multiprocessor system-on-chip (MPSoC).
Besides, to ensure a long processor lifetime and to maintain
a safe chip temperature are also important for multiprocessor
manufactures under deep submicrometer process technologies.
This paper presents a Mixed Integer Linear Programming
(MILP) model to determine the mapping and scheduling of realtime applications onto embedded MPSoC platforms, such that
the total energy consumption is minimized with the lifetime
reliability constraint and the temperature threshold constraint
satisfied. We develop a lightweight temperature model that can
be integrated in the MILP model to predict the chip temperature
accurately and efficiently. By exploiting the Dynamic Voltage
and Frequency Scaling (DVFS) capability of modern processors,
processor voltage/frequency assignment is also considered in our
MILP model. Extensive performance evaluations on synthetic
and real-world applications demonstrate the effectiveness of
the proposed approach. Our MILP model achieves an average
reduction of 19.09% and 28.53% total energy in comparison with
two state-of-the-art techniques on the basis of guaranteeing the
safe chip temperature and system lifetime reliability.
Index Terms—Multiprocessor system-on-chip, lifetime reliability, temperature model, real-time application mapping and
scheduling, energy optimization.

I. I NTRODUCTION
MultiProcessor System-on-Chip (MPSoC) is being widely
used in various embedded application domains like telecommunication (e.g., smart phones), transportation (e.g., car electronics) and industrial automation (e.g., process control). However, though have brought MPSoCs with enhanced functionality and improved performance, technology scaling and the
ever-increasing demand for system performance have resulted
in high power densities and increasing chip temperatures in
current circuits. Both of them make failure mechanisms serious
threats for reliable MPSoCs [1]. Chip temperature significantly
affects the device lifespan and the circuit soft error rate, e.g.,
a 10-15◦ C temperature increase can result in a 2× reduction
in the lifespan of device [2], and a 216% increase in soft
error rate is observed on c17 as the temperature climbs from
25◦ C to 125◦ C) [3]. Moreover, the leakage power grows
exponentially with the increasing temperature in the nanometer
era [4], and high chip temperature also requires effective
cooling and packaging solutions to ensure reliable runtime
operation, resulting in a significant increase in cooling and
packaging costs.

To obtain the chip temperature profiles for thermal-aware
managements on MPSoC platforms, numerous temperature
estimation techniques have been proposed [5], [6], [7]. Most
of them suffer from either inaccurate estimation precision or
expensive computation cost (time consuming) during design
space exploration. Inaccurate prediction (an underestimation or
overestimation of chip temperature) would result in overheated
processor cores and further affect the system reliability or
reduced performance at runtime. Meanwhile, the temperature
estimation process has to be computationally efficient.
In addition, ensuring a long processor lifetime by limiting
wear-out related failures is also a critical requirement for all
multiprocessor manufactures in deep submicrometer process
technologies. As demonstrated in [8], the average Mean Time
To Failure (MTTF) of a contemporary superscalar processor
drops by about 4× from 180nm to 65nm technology nodes.
The ITRS has identified lifetime reliability as a growing
challenge for users and designers of all types of integrated
circuits [9]. It is expected that, in the near future, the design
cost and effort required for ensuring lifetime reliability will
be as high as for classic design constraints, e.g., timing and
power consumption. Therefore, it is essential to consider the
lifetime reliability issue in embedded system design.
Energy optimization has been a major factor to be considered for MPSoCs at system-level design. DVFS techniques
are widely explored for energy optimization. Considering both
the energy consumption and the system reliability in terms of
lifetime reliability or chip temperature on MPSoCs, extensive
researches have been proposed [10], [11], [12], [13], [14],
[15]. However, existing works on energy-efficient application
mapping and scheduling techniques only consider thermal
control or lifetime reliability constraint separately. On-chip
temperature profile and system lifetime reliability affect each
other and complement each other. On the one hand, chip
temperature is necessary to be considered to guarantee system
lifetime reliability; many failure mechanisms are exponentially
dependent on chip temperature. Considering chip temperature
ensures that the chip temperature is always below safe threshold at any moment. Although temperature-aware methods
[16], [15], [17] are effective in reducing temperature, they
may not enhance and may even hamper the system lifetime
reliability that is also related to other aging factors, such
as current density and voltage. On the other hand, ensuring
processors’ lifetime cannot perfectly prevent chip temperature
from violating the safe threshold, since the system lifetime
reliability is estimated based on the whole scheduling period
and is a result of the accumulation effect of multiple factors.
The complicated relationship between chip temperature and

2

system lifetime reliability requires that both of them should be
considered explicitly for the system design of energy-efficient
techniques.
In this paper, we propose an effective Mixed Integer Linear Programming (MILP)-based approach to conduct energyefficient application mapping and scheduling on MPSoC
platforms, which explicitly takes both the transient impact
caused by chip temperature and the duration impact resulted
from lifetime reliability on MPSoCs into consideration. To
estimate the chip temperature and determine the effect of
temperature on chip’s aging accurately and efficiently, we
develop a lightweight temperature model that is integrated
in the MILP model. Our MILP model minimizes the energy
consumption of applications by judiciously determining their
mapping and scheduling based on voltage/frequency scaling
techniques. Chip temperature is guaranteed below the safe
threshold and the chip lifetime would be at least the predefined
target value. The accuracy and efficiency of the temperature
model are verified by comparing with existing temperature
estimation techniques; Compared to the HotSpot simulator
that has been widely recognized for the architecture-level
temperature modeling on MPSoCs, an average of over 99%
accuracy is presented. The effectiveness and scalability of
the proposed MILP-based technique are verified by a huge
set of synthetic applications and real-world applications on
different floorplans. The experimental results show that our
approach reduces 24.3% reliability threshold violations on
average and achieves 19.09% and 28.53% total energy savings
in comparison with two state-of-the-art techniques.
The contributions of this paper are listed as follows:
• An accurate and efficient temperature model is developed,
which achieves an average of over 99% temperature prediction accuracy with greatly reduced computation time,
compared to a widely recognized temperature simulator.
• A systematical approach for energy-efficient application
mapping and scheduling on MPSoC platforms is proposed, in which chip temperature profile and lifetime
reliability issue are explicitly taken into consideration
simultaneously and are well controlled. Performance evaluations on extensive synthetic and real-world applications
prove the effectiveness of the proposed approach.
The rest of this paper is organized as follows: Section II
discusses related work; Section III presents the system models and reliability modeling techniques; Section IV uses an
example to illustrate the motivation of this paper, and then formulates the problem studied in this paper; Section V presents
the lightweight temperature model, and Section VI presents
the MILP-based energy optimization technique; Experimental
results and concluding remarks are provided in Section VII
and Section VIII, respectively.
II. R ELATED W ORK
A significant body of work has been published on DVFSaware task allocation and scheduling techniques for thermal
control or lifetime optimization. Chantem et al. [18], [14],
[15], [19] tried to minimize the maximum temperature of
MPSoCs by utilizing the DVFS technique, subjecting to the

real-time constraint of applications. Huang and Xu et al. [10]
proposed a simulated annealing based algorithm to minimize
energy for MPSoC considering the lifetime reliability constraint. Das et al. [11] tried to maximize system lifetime while
maintaining the energy consumption under a given energy
budget using DVFS and task replication. Hanumaiah et al.
[12] maximized the overall energy efficiency yet maintained
the safe chip temperature by considering DVFS and thread
migration. Zhuravlev et al. [20] surveyed the related scheduling techniques performing energy-efficient computation. These
works have mainly tackled one aspect of the complex scenario:
by acting on DVFS and processor frequency assignment for
optimizing the trade-off between performance and energy, or
by accurately mapping the application to improve the system
lifetime. However, none of these works takes both the chip
temperature control and the system lifetime reliability into
consideration explicitly.
Both system-level lifetime design methods and thermalaware design techniques require accurate thermal estimation techniques. A thermal emulation framework for fieldprogrammable gate arrays (FPGAs) was introduced in [5],
which allows designers to extract a number of critical statistics
from processor cores, memories and interconnection systems.
Another temperature model developed by Dhodapkar et al. [7],
called TEMPEST, models the temperature directly using an
equivalent RC circuit containing only a single RC pair for the
entire chip. HotSpot [6] is an automated temperature model
which uses the duality between heat flow and electrical circuit
phenomena. Each core on the die is divided into several blocks,
with the same division being applied to the Thermal Interface
Material (TIM). The heat spreader and the heat sink are also
divided into multiple blocks. For a processor with n cores
and m functional units for each core, there will be n ∗ m
block in the die, another n ∗ m blocks in the TIM and 14
blocks in the package according to the model. HotSpot has
been widely recognized for the architecture-level temperature
modeling on MPSoCs. However, the computation efficiency of
HotSpot limits its application for quick temperature estimation
during design space exploration.
III. P RELIMINARIES
In this section, we introduce the application model, system
model, power model, and lifetime reliability model used in
this paper in turn.
A. Application model
We use a Data Flow Graph (DFG) to model the application
to be executed in the system. A DFG G =⟨T , E⟩ is a directed
acyclic graph (DAG), where T = {τ1 , τ2 , ..., τn } is the set
of nodes, and E ⊆ T × T is the set of edges that defines
the precedence relations among the nodes in T . Each node
τi ∈ T represents a task to be executed. Each edge in the
graph (eij ∈ E) indicates that task τj is dependent on τi . A
deadline L is associated with a DFG G, which indicates that
the application G has to be completed before L. We assume the
worst-case execution times (WCETs) of the tasks are known
a priori.

B. System Model and Power Model
We consider a MPSoC architecture consisting of a set of
processor cores, which is defined as M. For each processor,
we can assign different frequencies and voltages at any time.
Unlike the static frequency assignment with fixed operating
frequency at all time, the dynamic case that varies the frequencies and voltages gains a better performance and increases
the energy efficiency. We assume that the processor cores are
architecturally identical and the only source of heterogeneity
is that the processors can operate at different voltage and
frequency levels, as assumed in [21], [22].
Each processor has K discrete voltage settings V =
{V1 , V2 , ..., VK } (in descending order). Each voltage setting Vk
can be associated with a computation speed vk in terms of cycles/second. The energy consumption for executing any τi ∈ T
at speed vk can be expressed as Ei,k = P w(Vk , vk ) × ti,k ,
where P w(Vk , vk ) is the power consumption under voltage
Vk and speed vk , and ti,k is the execution time of task τi with
speed vk . Assuming the tasks execute up to their WCETs and
W CETi is given as the execution time of task τi in the default
(highest) processor frequency, the WCET for τi running at
processor speed vk is computed as: ti,k = W CETi ×(v1 /vk ).
A processor core has two operating modes: an active mode,
in which it is able to execute instructions, and a sleep mode,
in which it ceases working and rests with minimized power
consumption. The processor cores consume both dynamic
power and leakage power while executing instructions, and
only consume leakage power during idle periods. The leakage
power in a sleep mode is 3% of the leakage power in the
active mode running in the lowest frequency [23]. Dynamic
power dominates the power consumption of processors in
micrometer CMOS technology. While leakage power increases
its percentage in total power consumption as chip density
increases. Both dynamic power consumption and static power
consumption need to be considered in overall energy optimization of the system. The power consumption under different
voltage/frequency settings are similar to the model in [24],
[25].
C. Lifetime reliability model
In this paper, we focus on two critical failure factors, i.e., the
electromigration (EM) and time dependent dielectric breakdown (TDDB), in the lifetime reliability model. Various failure
mechanisms have been extensively studied in the literature,
such as interconnect shorts/opens during fabrication that can
be weeded out during the manufacturing test and burn-in
process, radiation effects that do not fundamentally damage the
circuit and are not viewed as lifetime reliability threats, and the
hard errors of intrinsic failures that are permanent once they
manifest. The most representative hard errors include TDDB
in the gate oxides and EM in the interconnects [1]. Both of
them are widely recognized to decrease the reliability of chips
(integrated circuits (ICs)) significantly.
Electromigration (EM) is the transport of material resulted
from the gradual movement of the ions in the conducting path
due to the momentum transfer between conducting electrons
and diffusing metal atoms. It is a typical reason that will lead

to the permanent faults such as opening of metal lines/contacts,
shortening between adjacent metal lines, etc. The MTTF of a
wire, taking EM into consideration, is as follows [26]:

B. Problem formulation
Based on the above analysis, the problem addressed in
this paper is formulated as follows: Given the floorplan of a
MPSoC containing a set of processor cores M, an application
represented with a task graph G =⟨T , E⟩, its associated deadline L, a thermal threshold T H, and a target system lifetime
LF , determine a non-preemptive schedule of the application
and the corresponding voltage/frequency level of the core
running each task, such that the total energy consumption is
minimized, under the constraints that tasks are finished before
deadlines, the chip runs below thermal threshold T H, and the
system lifetime is guaranteed to be no less than LF .
V. L IGHTWEIGHT TEMPERATURE MODEL
In this section, we present a lightweight temperature model
for on-chip temperature prediction.
The heating/cooling process of MPSoCs is complicated
and dynamic. As the approaches in [15], [36], [13] show,
inspired by the duality between heat flow and electrical circuit
phenomenon, the heating/cooling process in an MPSoC can be
approximately modeled as an equivalent first-order RC circuit
by applying Fourier’s Law. The thermal coefficients can be
obtained in [37].
For a MPSoC platform with a set of processor cores M,
each core is modeled as a discrete thermal element. Heat sink
and heat spreader layers are on top of the cores. We combine
the heat sink layer and heat spreader layer as a whole and still
call it heat sink layer for convenience, similar to the model
in [6], [15]. Heat sink layer can also be modeled as multiple
thermal elements whose partitioning corresponds to the layout
of the cores. Define H as the set of heat sink elements in the
MPSoC according to their partition.
The heat transfer among cores and heat sink elements is
quite related to the power consumption of processor cores,
the ambient temperature, and the temperature of neighboring
thermal elements. When the power consumption of each core
is the same, the percentage of energy converted to heat among
cores is very low, around 3%. Theoretically, there is no heat
transfer between the cores. However, in the situation where a
core is idle and its neighbors are running tasks, a relatively
high percentage of energy is converted to heat, and the core
absorbs heat from its neighbors. A part of the heat is dissipated
through heat sink, and the rest is absorbed by itself which leads
to a rise of its temperature.
Let Ce be the thermal capacitance for any thermal element
e ∈ M ∪ H. Let Rm,l be the lateral thermal resistance
between core m and l for any m, l ∈ M. Let Rh,g be the
thermal resistance between two heat sink elements h and g
for any h, g ∈ H. Let Rh,A be the vertical resistance of a
heatsink elements h ∈ H to the ambient. For each task-tocore assignment, P w(τ, m) indicates the power consumption
of core m when executing task τ . The temperature of thermal
element e ∈ M ∪ H at time t is denoted by T (t, e). The
temperature of each thermal element can be expressed as a
function of its power consumption, the ambient temperature,
and the temperatures of neighboring thermal elements. The
heating/cooling process by Fourier’s Law can be formulated
as follows:

To make the temperature model more robust to different
configurations, we add two scaling factors, f c and f h, in
the model, as (5) and (6) suggest. Both f c and f h are
applied for adjusting vertical thermal resistances. The vertical
thermal resistance captures heat flow from one layer to the
next, moving from the die through the package and eventually
into the air. For example, Rm,h counts the amount of heat
flowing from core m into the heat sink elements h that is
directly above the core m. The value of the vertical thermal
resistance of a specific architecture generally indicates the heat
dissipation rate. The lateral thermal resistance captures the heat
diffusion between adjacent thermal elements within a layer.
It indicates the temperature difference between the adjacent
thermal elements. It is observed that the temperature value
estimated by [15] is lower than that obtained from HotSpot.
This is because the heat spreader layer is omitted and the
divided equivalent circuit is not accurate enough, though the
temperature differences among adjacent thermal elements are
well presented. To offset the accuracy loss from imprecise
vertical thermal resistance, two scaling factors are used to
adjust the vertical thermal resistance empirically in an efficient
manner. The two scaling factors can be calculated easily as
described in [4].

B. Evaluation of the MILP-based Approach
We conduct extensive performance evaluations on both
synthetic and real-world benchmarks to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed MILP-based application mapping
and scheduling approach.
In this paper, the temperature threshold is set to 70◦ C, and
the targeted chip lifetime is set to 10 years. We calibrate the
constants in each failure equation to give a system MTTF of 30

years at nominal temperature and we use the same constants
for all the experiments. The parameters of the electromigration
failure model are set as follows: the cross-sectional area of
conductor Ac = 6.4 × 10−8 cm2 , the current density for the
original voltage level J = 1.5 × 106 A/cm2 , and the activation
energy Ea = 0.48 eV [1]. The parameters of the TDDB failure
model are set as follows: a = 78, b = −0.081, X = 0.759ev,
Y = −66.8evK and Z = −8.37e − 4ev/K [30].
To illustrate the effectiveness and performance of our MILPbased approach, a large number of experiments are performed
by comparing our approach with a simulated annealing-based
technique [1] and a heuristic algorithm ThermalSched [15].
Simulated annealing-based algorithm is an effective technique which can be devised to solve application mapping
and scheduling problems. Many researchers devise SA-based
algorithm to obtain the results effectively and efficiently [42].
The MILP model is solved by Gurobi v5.60 [43] with CVX
2.1 in the Matlab, which is designed to be the fastest, most
powerful solver available for MILP problems. We set the running time limit of the MILP model to two hours and ten hours
for the synthetic benchmarks and real-world benchmarks, respectively. Considering the heavy time overhead of this formal
method, we can relax its optimality to obtain a sub-optimal yet
highly acceptable application mapping and scheduling solution
more efficiently. In this paper, a sub-optimal resultis also
generated from the MILP solver by setting the current relative
MIP (Mixed Integer Programming) optimality gap to 5%. The
gap is computed as (ObjBound − ObjV al)/|ObjV al|, where
ObjBound and ObjV al are the MIP objective bound and
incumbent solution objective, respectively. The Gurobi solver
will terminate (with an optimal/sub-optimal result) when the
relative gap between the lower and upper objective bound is
less than the gap times the upper bound.
Both the simulated annealing algorithm and the ThermalSched algorithm are run in the Matlab. The parameters
for the simulated annealing approach are set as follows:
initial temperature = 100, cooling rate = 0.99, and ending
temperature = 10−5 . The ThermalSched algorithm [15] is
extended accordingly to be applied to the proposed problem.
Specifically, the earliest start time and the latest start time
of a given task are computed using a middle voltage level

that the optimal result obtained with MILP model reduces
energy by 19.09% compared to SA-based algorithm, and
28.53% compared to ThermalSched. The time needed to obtain
an optimal result spans from less than 1 minute (for autocor
and tde pp) to several hours. The optimal results cannot be
obtained within 10 hours for H.264 decoder under the 3×3
and 4×4 mesh-based MPSoC. For the suboptimal results
obtained with MILP formulation, it is quite close to the
optimal results. Specifically, an average of 16.29% and 27%

energy reduction is achieved compared to SA-based algorithm
and ThermalSched, respectively. Moreover, the time needed for
suboptimal results is much shorter than it for optimal results,
ranging from less than 1 minute to at most 45 minutes. Note
that the time needed for the MILP solver depends on a number
of factors, and it cannot simply be measured by the size of the
chip floorplan or the application. In the case of the “fmradio”,
the time needed for the 3×3 mesh-based floorplan is much
shorter than it needed for the 2×2 mesh-based floorplan. This
is normal phenomenon in the case of finding optimal solution
using MILP model.
In summary, our approach exhibits high effectiveness and
good performance compared to the state-of-the-art techniques,
which achieves energy minimization for real-time application
mapping and scheduling at design time, with the safe chip
temperature and system lifetime reliability constraints satisfied.
VIII. C ONCLUSION
This paper has proposed a high-level energy optimization
framework, in which a MILP formulation is proposed for
mapping and scheduling real-time applications onto MPSoCs.
Combining DVFS techniques, the MILP formulation minimizes the total energy consumption with both thermal threshold constraint and system lifetime reliability constraint satisfied. A lightweight temperature model has also been employed
to efficiently yet accurately estimate the thermal distribution of
MPSoCs in the MILP formulation. The accuracy and efficiency
of the temperature model have been verified by comparing
with existing temperature models. Its prediction accuracy is
improved by as much as 46% than the existing temperature
model, and an average of over 99% accuracy is provided
with greatly reduced computation time comparing with the
architecture-level HotSpot simulator. The effectiveness and
scalability of the MILP-based technique have been further
verified. We have conducted extensive experiments on a large
number of synthetic and real-world applications on different
floorplans. The results show that our approach reduces on
average 19.09% and 28.53% total energy consumption in
comparison with two state-of-the-art techniques, with the tem-
